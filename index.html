The Premise

Games are the most sophisticated spatial storytelling we have. A level designer moves you through a building without a single word of instruction — using light, threshold, compression, sightline, and scale. That is architecture doing narrative work.

This semester you will take a shipped game apart with an architect's drawings, then build a world of your own using the same tools. Your story will be told through one architectural verb, one building part, and one drawing medium — drawn at random, non-negotiable, on the first day.

Three Rules, All Semester

Rule 01

No text. No dialogue. No UI.

If the story doesn't survive the removal of every word, the architecture isn't doing the work. Environmental storytelling only.

Rule 02

The camera obeys your medium.

Drawn axonometric? Your UE5 camera is orthographic and locked. Drawn section? You cut the model and sit in the cut plane. No swooping hero shots to rescue a weak idea.

Rule 03

One screenshot a week.

Post to #devlog on Discord every week, no exceptions, however ugly. This is how real studios work, and it counts toward participation.

The Draw: Verb × Part × Medium

On Day 1 you draw three cards. They are yours for the semester — no trades. Example: Compressing & Releasing × Stair × Section.

1. Architectural Verb

What the space does to a body.

  • Framing a View
  • Guiding Movement
  • Creating Thresholds
  • Compressing & Releasing
  • Ascending & Descending
  • Sheltering & Exposing
  • Revealing & Concealing
  • Scaling & Dominating
  • Grounding & Floating
  • Reflecting & Distorting

2. Building Part

Your only instrument. Model it, twist it, tell with it.

  • Wall / Façade
  • Floor / Ground Plane
  • Ceiling / Roof
  • Door / Opening
  • Window / Aperture
  • Stair / Ramp
  • Column / Structure
  • Pathway / Corridor

3. Medium (NEW)

Governs your UE5 camera for the whole semester.

  • Section — cut the model, sit in the cut plane (Inside, Limbo)
  • Plan — top-down; story via choreography (Death's Door)
  • Axonometric — orthographic, locked (Monument Valley, Hades)
  • Elevation — flat locked camera, parallax layers (GRIS)
  • Worm's-Eye / RCP — the camera only looks up
  • Exploded Assembly — the building comes apart as the story advances
  • Collage / Montage — layered plates (Kentucky Route Zero)
  • Panel Grid — simultaneous cameras (Gorogoa)
  • One-Point Perspective — locked vanishing point
  • Cutaway Diorama — dollhouse; walls removed

Assignment 01: The Teardown

Weeks 1–3. You will take your group's game apart in four layers — design architecture first, mechanics second — and present it as a GDC-style postmortem. This is your Demo grade (10%).

Layer 1

Design Architecture — the macro

How is the world shaped? Critical path vs. optional space. Gates and keys. Hub-and-spoke, open field, or corridor. Where does it fork and re-converge?

Layer 2

Spatial Grammar — the micro

Landmarks and lures. Sightlines. Thresholds. Compression and release. How does the game teach you where to go without a marker? What is the material and colour language for “climbable,” “danger,” “story here”?

Layer 3

Mechanics — and what they mean

What verbs does the player own? What is the core loop? Then the real question: how does the mechanic carry the theme? In Edith Finch the mechanic changes with every story because each is a different mind. In Stray you are low to the ground because the world was not built for you.

Layer 4

Pacing — the rhythm

Intensity over time. Combat, quiet, cutscene, traversal. Where are the silences, and what do they do?

Six Required Diagrams

Architect's drawings, applied to a game. Hand or digital — but they must be drawn, not screenshotted.

01

Level graph — nodes and edges, gates marked, critical path in red

02

Redrawn plan of one level, annotated with landmarks and sightlines

03

Section through a key vertical moment — what you see, and when

04

Sightline / reveal cone — the choreography of a single reveal

05

Core loop diagram — verb → feedback → reward → new verb

06

Intensity curve — pacing across 30 minutes of play

+

Moment map (bonus) — one 30-second sequence, broken down frame by frame

The payoff: in Week 8 you will draw these same six diagrams for your own project. You dissect a shipped game, then design with the same instrument.
