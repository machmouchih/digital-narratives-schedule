Course Description

How does interaction with space shape a narrative?

This course explores spatial narratives in the unbuilt world. From games, movies, AR/VR, to metaverse, students will explore the idea of space in the digital realm. The discussion will be on: How does one interact with space? And how does that interaction with space shape a spatial narrative?

This workshop encompasses both theoretical readings on spatial narratives as well as learning and working with three-dimensional software in order to compose a holistic perspective of three-dimensional narratives in the virtual realm. Students will be required to play virtual games, study their spaces and narratives, and create digital narratives.

This semester the game analysis is expanded. Students take their assigned game apart in four layers, starting with its design architecture and moving to its mechanics, and document it in six diagrams. Rhino and Unreal begin in Week 1 and run alongside the analysis. Each student is also assigned a medium, which governs both the drawings and the camera setup in Unreal for the rest of the semester. The second half of the semester is spent designing and building an original game mechanic.

Choose One

Building Part

Your primary tool for storytelling. One part, worked hard, for the whole semester. Everything else in the scene supports it.

  • Wall / Façade The barrier and the face. What it hides on one side, what it announces on the other.
  • Floor / Ground Plane What carries the body. Level change, material underfoot, and what lies below.
  • Ceiling / Roof The overhead condition. Shelter, weight, and access to sky.
  • Door / Opening The controlled passage. Who may pass, when, and at what cost.
  • Window / Aperture The framed view. What is shown, what is withheld, and from where.
  • Stair / Ramp Movement through height. Effort, rhythm, and the moment of arrival.
  • Column / Structure The order of the space. Rhythm, grid, and what the building admits about how it stands.
  • Pathway / Corridor The enforced route. Duration, compression, and the pace it imposes.

The Medium

Your drawing convention is your camera

Architecture tells stories through drawing conventions, and games use the same ones. Monument Valley is an axonometric. Inside is a section. Gorogoa is a panel grid. Each student is assigned one convention. It sets the drawings you produce and the camera you build in Unreal Engine. A locked view cannot be fixed with camera movement, so the space has to carry the narrative.

Orthographic

  • Section The model is cut and the camera sits in the cut plane. Inside, Limbo
  • Plan Top down, narrative carried by choreography of movement. Death's Door
  • Axonometric Orthographic and locked, with no vanishing point. Monument Valley, Hades
  • Elevation A flat, fixed camera with narrative built in parallax layers. GRIS

Constructed

  • Worm's-Eye / RCP The camera only looks up.
  • Exploded Assembly The building comes apart as the narrative advances.
  • Cutaway Diorama Walls removed, the interior seen whole.
  • One-Point Perspective A fixed vanishing point and a symmetrical frame.

Composite

  • Collage / Photomontage Narrative assembled in layered plates. Kentucky Route Zero
  • Panel Grid Several simultaneous cameras read together. Gorogoa
  • Text / Typography Language placed in space as inscription, signage, or projection. Letterforms are built, sited, and lit like any other element. The Stanley Parable, NaissanceE

Assignment · Weeks 1–3

Game Space Analysis

Running alongside the Rhino and Unreal workshops. Play your group's game, take it apart in four layers, and present the analysis in Week 3. The question is not whether the game is good. The question is how it is built, and whether you can show it in drawings. This presentation is the Demo portion of your grade.

Layer 01

Design Architecture: how is the world structured?

Critical path against optional space. Gates and the keys that open them. Hub, open field, or corridor. Where the path forks and where it returns.

Layer 02

Spatial Grammar: how does the space instruct without instructions?

Landmarks, sightlines, thresholds, compression and release. Turn the waypoint marker off and document what still tells you where to go. Every game builds a color and material language for climbable, dangerous, and worth your attention.

Layer 03

Game Mechanics: what can the player do, and what does it mean?

The verbs the player is given and the core loop they sit inside. Then: how does the mechanic carry the theme? In What Remains of Edith Finch the mechanic changes with each story because each is a different mind. In Stray the player is held low to the ground because the world was not built for a cat.

Layer 04

Pacing: how does the experience rise and fall?

Intensity across time: conflict, traversal, quiet, cutscene. Note where the silences are placed and what they do.

Six Required Diagrams

  1. Level graph. Nodes and edges, with gates marked and the critical path distinguished.
  2. Plan of one level, redrawn and annotated with landmarks and sightlines.
  3. Section through a significant vertical moment, showing what becomes visible and when.
  4. Sightline diagram of a single reveal, drawn as cones of vision.
  5. Core loop diagram. Action, feedback, reward, and the next action.
  6. Intensity curve across thirty minutes of play.

In Week 8 you will draw these same six diagrams for your own project.

Assignment · Weeks 8–11

Your Game Mechanic

In the first half of the semester you documented how other people's mechanics work. In the second half you build one. Every student designs a single core mechanic, specifies it, implements it in Blueprints, and tests it on a classmate.

Mechanic Specification

One page, written in Week 8, before any Blueprint work begins.

  • The verb What can the player do that they could not do before?
  • Input How is it triggered, and how does the player learn that without being told?
  • Feedback What changes in the world so the player knows it worked?
  • Limits What stops it, and what does the limit mean?
  • Meaning What does this mechanic say that a cutscene could not?

One mechanic, built properly, beats three that half work. The mechanic is tested in Week 11 by a classmate with no instructions. If they never find it, it does not work yet.

Workflow

A.I. in the Unreal pipeline

A.I. tools are part of the pipeline this semester, taught in Weeks 2, 4, 9, and 10. They are used for production, not for the thinking. The diagrams, the mechanic specification, and the narrative are yours.

Week 02

Previsualization

Image and video generation for moodboards, concept frames, and testing an idea before it is modeled.

Week 04

Asset Generation

Text and image to 3D for props and secondary geometry. Generated meshes come in dirty and are cleaned in Rhino. Your Building Part is modeled by you.

Week 09

Blueprint Scripting

Drafting, reading, and debugging Blueprint logic with an assistant. Useful for syntax and structure, unreliable on node-level specifics. You still have to understand the graph.

Week 10

Texture, Motion & Sound

Material and texture generation, video-based motion capture into UE5, and generated ambience.

Document your use of A.I. tools: what you generated, what you kept, and what you had to fix. Follow the BAC academic integrity policy on attribution.
